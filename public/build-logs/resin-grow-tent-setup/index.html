Why I'm setting it up this way

I do plenty of FDM printing, but resin adds a cleanup path I need to plan for: letting prints drip, washing and drying them, curing them, and handling the contaminated tools. I know how easy it is to grab a tool and think, “I'm pretty sure that's clean.” I want resin tools to stay in their own area, with those steps close together, so I don't have to rely on memory or carry uncured resin across the shop. That's why I'm starting with the workspace and workflow before I think about printer upgrades.

October 10, 2026Update

First things inside the tent

The tent isn't empty anymore. The light is an old fish tank light, not a UV light. It has no power switch of its own, so I added an external switch with a red rocker on its black cord and zip-tied it to the tent frame near the door. That lets me turn the light on and off from the door without touching the surge protector. Extension cords are coiled and hung on the wall nearby, and a black folding table is set up inside. The table is a temporary measure so I can get up and running sooner rather than later; the standing desk is still the plan for the main work surface.

Nothing in these photos has been tested for resin work. At the time, there was still no exhaust, wash station, or printer in here. My plan then was to build the exhaust before using resin.

One note on the cords: I coiled and hung them on the wall to keep them off the floor. The orange one is a 25 ft Harbor Freight cord, 16 gauge, and it's the only cord whose gauge I know for sure. The other cord is the one on the light, which gets replaced when the light does. Coiled cords hold heat, so I'll uncoil them before anything that draws real power runs through them.

Supplies that arrived. I bought four more items for the workspace, and they've been delivered. These are plain links, not affiliate links:

  • Inline extension cord switch: the red rocker I first added to the old fish-tank light because it had no switch, then reused with the Braun LED.
  • 2-pack of 45 oz pickle jars: for dirty wash storage, an idea I picked up from a YouTube video.
  • 36.2" × 24" extra large silicone mat
  • SUNLU 1000G ABS-Like Resin: unopened, in its bottle, stored inside the house for now.

I haven't tested any of them in this workspace yet, so this is a record of what I bought, not a recommendation. The resin needed a safety data sheet before anything gets poured, so I pulled it.

I also bought Hardy 9 mil nitrile gloves. I haven't found compatibility data for them against this resin and IPA, so I don't want to assume they're the right choice just because they're nitrile. That's something I still need to settle before I use them for resin work.

Next step: plastic on the floor and the table. This is where the two layers of plastic from the floor-protection plan go in, along with a covering for the folding table. To keep the plastic from shifting, I plan to print two clip designs from Printables: the 22mm grow tent clip collection and a tablecloth clip. I haven't printed or tested either one yet, so I don't know how well they'll hold.

The folding table and the tent floor now have plastic on them, and the Elegoo Saturn is sitting on the plastic-covered table. White clips hold the plastic at the table's edge. The next photo shows those parts in place: the empty vat is installed, and the build plate is raised above it. The Wi-Fi antenna is attached, and I got Wi-Fi set up on the Saturn on October 11. There's no resin in the vat, and I still haven't worked through the remaining setup checks, so the printer isn't ready to print yet.

I picked the Braun 5500 lumen, 4 ft linkable LED hanging shop light, Harbor Freight item 59506. This is a plain link, not an affiliate link. The shelf photo shows the light I chose. Its listing gives 55 W at 120 V, an on/off metal pull chain, and two chains with S-hooks; the box says 4000K cool white. Those are the manufacturer's numbers, not anything I've measured, and I haven't confirmed whether it puts out UV.

After I hung the light, I reused the inline rocker switch from the old fish-tank light on the Braun's black cord. The switch is by the tent door, so I can turn the light on there instead of reaching around to find its pull chain. The orange cord is the tent's primary power feed. The white cord looped below is excess length from the surge protector's cord, which is zip-tied nearby.

October 12, 2026Update

Planning the first print

Do as I say, not as I do. I'd tell someone else to wait until the exhaust is ready. Building it properly is going to take time and money, though, and I have new machines and want to play with them, so I'm planning a few prints before it's ready. I'll keep the printer lid on while it prints, crack the garage door, and stay out of the space while it's running. I'll need to open IPA to wash the prints afterward, so this plan still involves IPA in the garage without the finished exhaust. This doesn't give the tent exhaust or prove the setup is safe. It's how I'm choosing to proceed while I work out the proper system, not a safe setup or instructions to copy. I'll update this log with what happens.

A move I wouldn't recommend

When moving my resin equipment, I left it in my truck for a day before getting it into the garage. The machines had been emptied for the move, and it reached about 85°F that day. I wouldn't recommend leaving resin equipment in a vehicle on a hot day. My concern is sunlight reaching any uncured resin residue left on the equipment.

I don't know whether anything was affected: I haven't run either machine yet. I'll inspect the vats and build plates before use and record any problems I find. I can't call the move harmless or claim that heat damaged the machines.

I've also had cured resin end up in places I didn't want it on another resin machine. That's why I'm planning a defined dirty zone before this workspace sees any resin.

What I'd do differently

I haven't used this setup yet, so I can't call this a finished lesson. I did start by asking where the printer goes; now I'm trying to answer where everything goes after the print.
