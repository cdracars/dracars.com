Why I'm setting it up this way

I do plenty of FDM printing, but resin adds a cleanup path I need to plan for: letting prints drip, washing and drying them, curing them, and handling the contaminated tools. I know how easy it is to grab a tool and think, “I'm pretty sure that's clean.” I want resin tools to stay in their own area, with those steps close together, so I don't have to rely on memory or carry uncured resin across the shop. That's why I'm starting with the workspace and workflow before I think about printer upgrades.

October 10, 2026Update

First things inside the tent

The tent isn't empty anymore. The light is an old fish tank light, not a UV light. It has no power switch of its own, so I added an external switch with a red rocker on its black cord and zip-tied it to the tent frame near the door. That lets me turn the light on and off from the door without touching the surge protector. Extension cords are coiled and hung on the wall nearby, and a black folding table is set up inside. The table is a temporary measure so I can get up and running sooner rather than later; the standing desk is still the plan for the main work surface.

Nothing in these photos has been tested for resin work. At the time, there was still no exhaust, wash station, or printer in here. My plan then was to build the exhaust before using resin.

One note on the cords: I coiled and hung them on the wall to keep them off the floor. The orange one is a 25 ft Harbor Freight cord, 16 gauge, and it's the only cord whose gauge I know for sure. The other cord is the one on the light, which gets replaced when the light does. Coiled cords hold heat, so I'll uncoil them before anything that draws real power runs through them.

Supplies that arrived. I bought four more items for the workspace, and they've been delivered:

  • Inline extension cord switch: the red rocker I first added to the old fish-tank light because it had no switch, then reused with the Braun LED.
  • 2-pack of 45 oz pickle jars: for dirty wash storage, an idea I picked up from a YouTube video.
  • 36.2" × 24" extra large silicone mat
  • SUNLU 1000G ABS-Like Resin: unopened, in its bottle, stored inside the house for now.

I haven't tested any of them in this workspace yet, so this is a record of what I bought, not a recommendation. The resin needed a safety data sheet before anything gets poured, so I pulled it.

I also bought Hardy 9 mil nitrile gloves. I haven't found compatibility data for them against this resin and IPA, so I don't want to assume they're the right choice just because they're nitrile. That's something I still need to settle before I use them for resin work.

Next step: plastic on the floor and the table. This is where the two layers of plastic from the floor-protection plan go in, along with a covering for the folding table. To keep the plastic from shifting, I plan to print two clip designs from Printables: the 22mm grow tent clip collection and a tablecloth clip. I haven't printed or tested either one yet, so I don't know how well they'll hold.

Planning the first print

Do as I say, not as I do. I'd tell someone else to wait until the exhaust is ready. Building it properly is going to take time and money, though, and I have new machines and want to play with them, so I'm planning a few prints before it's ready. I'll keep the printer lid on while it prints, crack the garage door, and stay out of the space while it's running. It's how I'm choosing to proceed while I work out the proper system, not a safe setup or instructions to copy. I'll update this log with what happens.

October 18, 2026Update

The exposure test

Before the calibration print below, I ran an exposure test on the Saturn: a set of small Cones of Calibration test pieces, one at each exposure setting from 2 to 4 in steps of 0.4. I laid them out on a shop towel next to their labels.

I cured the pieces one at a time in a Fungdo resin curing enclosure, a small fabric box with a UV light and a turntable inside. When I took the towel photo, the 2 and 2.4 pieces had already gone to the enclosure; the marks on the towel are just where they sat.

My favorite part is the turntable. It's solar powered, so it starts spinning on its own when the UV light comes on and stops when the light goes off. I think that's super cool.

October 19, 2026Update

A successful calibration print

The resin calibration prints worked. I printed TableFlip Foundry's Cones of Calibration V3 on the Saturn 4 Ultra in the SUNLU ABS-Like Dark Grey resin, and it came off with every feature I can see intact: the skull, the thin spike, the five cones under the roof, the mug with its open handle, and the SUCCESS lettering along the base. That lettering is part of the model, so it's the print's own verdict, not a measurement. It tells me this exposure is in a workable range; it doesn't tell me the settings are tuned or that the next print will go the same way.

For now, all my resin printing is on the Saturn. The power in the tent is mounted up high, and the Photon P1's cord won't reach it from the folding table. That changes once the standing desk is in, since it can raise the P1 higher.

October 19, 2026Update

Where the exposure landed

From the calibration prints, I've settled on a 2.0 s exposure per layer at 0.05 mm layer height, with 6 bottom layers at 25 s. The lowest exposure that still printed was between 1.6 and 1.7 s. At 2.3 s, the bridge still didn't print. That's a starting point for this resin on this printer, not a final tune.

October 19, 2026Update

Where I messed up

That success shot isn't the whole story, and this log is supposed to be honest, so here's the part I'd rather skip.

After the calibration prints, I thought everything was good and ran a mini as a final test. What I didn't know was that the mugs on two of the calibration prints, the 1.9 s and 2.3 s ones, had torn at one end, and a piece of mug had come off and stayed in the vat. I don't know yet why they tore; peel force and where the parts sat on the build plate are my main suspects. I didn't check the vat before starting the mini, and the mini failed. Checking the vat between prints is the kind of step I'd tell someone else not to skip, and I skipped it.

Trying to get the stuck pieces out, I ran the Saturn's vat-cleaning job. It exposes the full LCD, so it cured a layer of resin across the whole bottom of the vat, and then I couldn't figure out how to get that layer out.

Then I emptied the vat, and that's where it really went sideways. The SUNLU resin came with a little paper filter funnel, and I didn't realize it's meant to sit inside a real, rigid funnel. You'd expect the paper to collapse on its own, but it didn't; once resin was in it, it stood up fine. The problem was that the resin soaked through the paper and ran down the outside of the bottle, and I made a huge mess. My wife came out to help me clean it all up.

With the resin finally out of the vat, I was able to pull the cured layer out, clean the vat fully, and put everything back together.

Next, I'm going to take the vat off and run another full-screen exposure test to make sure the failed print didn't damage the LCD, then work through some routine maintenance before printing again.

So learning has been "fun." The lessons I've remembered best are usually the ones I learned the hard way.

My biggest takeaway from the calibrations, the failed mini, and the cleanup is that resin is slow and methodical. Don't get in a hurry, and don't skip steps. Take it slow and do it right and safe. That's different from FDM, which can be a little fast and loose if you want it to be because there's less at stake when something goes wrong. With resin, one skipped check led to a failed print, a cured layer stuck in the vat, and a big mess.

Drip mods I've already printed

I do plenty of FDM printing, and I've already printed drip mods for both resin machines. I printed these before any resin printing.

Update, 2026-10-19: I used one of the drip mods on the Saturn during the calibration prints, and it worked gloriously. It's a little tight, though, so I'm printing a new drip mod that stays on the build plate.

I'm also printing a replacement for the drip tray that normally comes with the Saturn. Mine was used and didn't come with one, and it drips onto the outside of the printer a lot. Every time I moved the build plate during the calibrations, resin dripped everywhere, and I was wiping constantly. The plate-mounted drip mod and the drip tray are both going on the printer queue tonight; I haven't printed or tested either yet.

A move I wouldn't recommend

When moving my resin equipment, I left it in my truck for a day before getting it into the garage. The machines had been emptied for the move, and it reached about 85°F that day. I wouldn't recommend leaving resin equipment in a vehicle on a hot day. My concern is sunlight reaching any uncured resin residue left on the equipment.

I don't know whether anything was affected; I hadn't run either machine when I wrote this. I'll inspect the vats and build plates before use and record any problems I find. I can't call the move harmless or claim that heat damaged the machines.

I've also had cured resin end up in places I didn't want it on another resin machine. That's why I'm planning a defined dirty zone before this workspace sees any resin.

What I'd do differently

I've only run an exposure test, a calibration print, and one failed mini in this setup, so I can't call this a finished lesson. I did start by asking where the printer goes; now I'm trying to answer where everything goes after the print.
